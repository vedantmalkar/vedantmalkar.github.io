Localization using UWBs

February 2026

In the world of indoor positioning and real-time location systems, Ultra-Wideband (UWB) technology has emerged as a game-changer. With centimeter-level accuracy and robust performance in challenging environments, UWB is revolutionizing how we track and localize objects in spaces where GPS simply doesn't work.

What is UWB?

Ultra-Wideband (UWB) is a radio technology that uses extremely short pulses across a very wide frequency spectrum, typically ranging from 3.1 to 10.6 GHz. Unlike traditional narrowband systems that concentrate their energy in a small frequency range, UWB spreads its signal across a bandwidth of at least 500 MHz.

The key advantages of UWB for localization include:

  • High Precision: Centimeter-level accuracy in distance measurements
  • Low Power Consumption: Short pulse durations mean efficient power usage
  • Multipath Resistance: Short pulses can distinguish between direct and reflected signals
  • Penetration Capability: Can work through walls and obstacles better than many alternatives
  • Security: Difficult to detect and jam due to low power spectral density

UWB measures distance by calculating the time it takes for a signal to travel between devices. Since radio waves travel at the speed of light (approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s), even nanosecond-level timing precision translates to centimeter-level position accuracy.

Two-Way Ranging (TWR)

Two-Way Ranging is one of the fundamental techniques used in UWB positioning systems. As the name suggests, it involves a bidirectional exchange of signals between two devices to measure the distance between them.

How TWR Works

The TWR process follows these steps:

  1. Device A (the tag) sends a poll message to Device B (the anchor)
  2. Device B receives the poll and, after a known delay, sends a response message back
  3. Device A receives the response and calculates the round-trip time
  4. By subtracting the known processing delays, the actual time-of-flight can be calculated
  5. Distance = (Time-of-Flight × Speed of Light) / 2

Key Advantage: TWR doesn't require clock synchronization between devices because it measures the round-trip time. Each device only needs an accurate local clock.

Limitations of TWR

While TWR is accurate and doesn't require synchronized clocks, it has some drawbacks:

  • Communication Overhead: Each distance measurement requires multiple message exchanges
  • Scalability Issues: In a system with many tags, each tag must communicate with multiple anchors, creating significant network traffic
  • Update Rate: The back-and-forth communication limits how frequently positions can be updated
  • Collision Problems: Multiple tags communicating simultaneously can cause message collisions

Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA)

Time Difference of Arrival takes a fundamentally different approach to UWB localization. Instead of measuring absolute distances, TDOA measures the difference in arrival times of a signal at multiple synchronized receivers (anchors).

How TDOA Works

The TDOA process is elegantly simple:

  1. A tag transmits a single UWB pulse
  2. Multiple synchronized anchors receive this pulse at slightly different times
  3. The time differences between arrivals at different anchor pairs are calculated
  4. Each time difference defines a hyperbola on which the tag must lie
  5. The intersection of multiple hyperbolas determines the tag's position

Mathematically, if a signal arrives at anchor A₁ at time t₁ and at anchor A₂ at time t₂, the time difference Δt = t₂ - t₁ corresponds to a constant difference in distances. This defines a hyperbola with foci at the two anchor positions.

Why TDOA is Better

TDOA has emerged as the superior approach for most real-time localization systems, particularly when tracking multiple objects. Here's why:

1. Scalability

TDOA scales beautifully with the number of tags. Since tags only transmit and don't need to receive responses, adding more tags to the system doesn't create the exponential communication overhead that TWR does. A single transmission from a tag can be processed by all anchors simultaneously.

2. Higher Update Rates

Without the need for bidirectional communication, TDOA systems can achieve much higher position update rates. Tags can transmit as frequently as needed, limited only by regulations and power constraints, not by communication protocol delays.

3. Simpler Tag Design

TDOA tags only need to transmit, making them simpler, cheaper, and more power-efficient than TWR tags that must both transmit and receive. This is particularly important for battery-powered wearable tags or asset tracking applications.

4. Reduced Network Congestion

The one-way nature of TDOA communication means less airtime usage and fewer message collisions. This makes the system more robust in environments with many tags or high-frequency updates.

5. Better Real-Time Performance

TDOA can provide near-instantaneous position updates because there's no waiting for response messages. This makes it ideal for applications requiring real-time tracking, such as robotics navigation or sports analytics.

The Trade-off: TDOA's main requirement is that all anchors must be precisely time-synchronized, typically using wired connections or high-precision clock synchronization protocols. This infrastructure requirement is the price paid for TDOA's superior scalability and performance.

Practical Applications

The advantages of TDOA-based UWB systems make them ideal for various applications:

  • Indoor Robotics: Autonomous mobile robots navigating warehouses or manufacturing facilities
  • Asset Tracking: Real-time tracking of equipment, inventory, or vehicles in large facilities
  • Sports Analytics: Tracking player positions in indoor sports with centimeter precision
  • AR/VR Systems: Precise position tracking for immersive experiences
  • Safety Systems: Monitoring personnel in hazardous industrial environments

Conclusion

While both TWR and TDOA are powerful UWB localization techniques, TDOA's superior scalability, higher update rates, and simpler tag requirements make it the preferred choice for modern real-time location systems. The infrastructure investment required for anchor synchronization is outweighed by the system-level benefits, particularly in applications requiring high-frequency updates or tracking of many objects simultaneously.

As UWB technology continues to mature and becomes integrated into consumer devices and industrial systems, TDOA-based positioning will likely become the standard approach for precise indoor localization.
